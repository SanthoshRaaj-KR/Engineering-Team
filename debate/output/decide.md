The **opposition** is more convincing.

### Why
The pro-motion side makes a strong case that AI will take over many repetitive entry-level tasks, especially in IT services, BPO, back-office support, QA, and basic data work. That is a real and important point. It also rightly notes that companies will want lower costs and higher productivity after 2026, which will put pressure on fresher hiring.

However, the opposition ultimately gives the stronger argument because it distinguishes between **reducing fresher hiring** and **replacing it entirely**. The motion claims replacement, but the evidence presented supports more of a **reshaping** or **reduction** of fresher roles, not their disappearance.

### Why the opposition wins
1. **Fresher roles are not only repetitive work**
   The opposition correctly points out that freshers are also hired to learn, adapt, communicate, and grow into future experienced employees. AI can automate tasks, but it cannot fully replace the human pipeline needed for judgment, accountability, client handling, and long-term leadership development.

2. **Companies need future talent**
   The opposition makes a strong strategic point: if companies stop hiring freshers, they damage their own future workforce. Senior talent has to come from somewhere. This is a compelling rebuttal to the idea that firms will simply replace freshers with AI and move on.

3. **AI creates new entry-level work**
   The pro side focuses on AI replacing entry-level tasks, but the opposition shows that new entry-level tasks will also emerge: checking AI outputs, managing data, tuning tools, handling exceptions, and supporting human-AI workflows. That weakens the claim of complete replacement.

4. **India’s market is too diverse for full automation**
   The opposition’s point about India’s scale and sector diversity is persuasive. Many firms, especially smaller ones, will not be able to fully automate junior work after 2026. Adoption will likely be uneven, meaning AI will change hiring patterns rather than eliminate fresher hiring altogether.

### Final judgment
The pro side proves that AI will **disrupt** fresher hiring and likely **reduce** it in some sectors. But the opposition more convincingly shows that the word **replace** is too extreme. The stronger conclusion is that AI will **transform** fresher hiring in India after 2026, not replace it.

**Decision: the opposition wins.**
