I strongly support the motion: **the rise of AI is going to replace fresher hiring in India after 2026**.

Here’s why this is highly likely:

### 1) Fresher roles are the easiest to automate
Most entry-level jobs in India are built around **repetitive, rule-based, and low-risk tasks**—exactly the kind of work AI is becoming very good at.  
Freshers are often hired for:
- data entry
- basic customer support
- report generation
- simple coding
- documentation
- QA testing
- HR screening
- backend process support

These tasks do not require deep experience. As AI tools improve, companies will increasingly ask: **Why hire and train a fresher for 6–12 months when AI can do the work faster, cheaper, and without turnover?**

### 2) Companies will prioritize productivity over training
After 2026, businesses will face stronger pressure to cut costs and increase output. Fresher hiring is expensive in hidden ways:
- training costs
- onboarding time
- supervision
- errors due to inexperience
- retention risk

AI reduces all of these. If one AI-powered employee can handle the workload of several freshers, companies will naturally reduce fresh hiring. This will be especially true in India’s IT services, BPO, support, operations, and back-office sectors.

### 3) AI is not just replacing jobs — it is replacing the need for many junior roles
Earlier, companies hired freshers because they needed a large human workforce to handle volume. Now AI can:
- write first drafts
- answer queries
- summarize documents
- generate code
- detect defects
- screen resumes
- analyze basic data

This means many “training-ground” jobs will disappear. And if the entry-level layer shrinks, the entire fresher hiring pipeline shrinks with it.

### 4) India is especially vulnerable because of its large graduate supply
India produces millions of graduates every year. But the number of truly high-skill jobs is much smaller. AI will widen this gap. When companies can use AI to do junior-level work, they will hire fewer freshers and demand only those who already have niche skills, practical experience, or AI-assisted productivity.

So the future won’t be “hire everyone and train them.” It will be **hire only the best, most job-ready candidates**.

### 5) After 2026, AI will be mature enough for real business deployment
We are already seeing AI move from experimentation to implementation. By and after 2026, AI will be more reliable, cheaper, and more deeply integrated into enterprise systems. That is the tipping point. Once AI becomes standard in workflows, the first jobs to be cut will be the ones that are easiest to digitize—freshers’ jobs.

### Conclusion
The reality is simple: **freshers are most vulnerable because they are hired for tasks AI can increasingly do better, faster, and cheaper**. After 2026, AI will not just assist fresher hiring in India—it will **replace a major share of it**.

So yes, the rise of AI is going to replace fresher hiring in India after 2026.
